Services

Estate & Legacy

Helping you pass on more than assets — with a plan that honors your intentions, supports the people and causes you care about, and works in concert with your own estate attorney.

What This Includes

The scope of our estate & legacy work

We help organize the financial side of your legacy and bring it into focus alongside your broader plan. Ktema does not draft legal documents or provide legal advice — that work belongs with your estate attorney, with whom we coordinate closely.

Wealth-transfer planning

We map how assets are expected to move to the next generation and identify where titling, account structure, or sequencing could be aligned more closely with your wishes — then bring those questions to your attorney.

Lifetime gifting strategy

We help you weigh the trade-offs of gifting during your lifetime — to family or to charity — and how those gifts interact with your cash flow, your portfolio, and your long-term goals.

Beneficiary & account-titling review

Beneficiary designations and how accounts are titled often control more than a will does. We review them across your accounts so they reflect your current intentions, not a form filled out years ago.

Charitable & philanthropic strategy

For clients who want to give with intention, we help structure the financial mechanics — when and what to give, and how giving fits the rest of the plan — in coordination with your attorney and tax advisor.

Coordination with your estate attorney

We work directly with the attorney you choose, sharing the financial picture so documents and strategy stay in step. We do not practice law or draft instruments — we make sure the planning is informed.

Multi-generational legacy planning

For families thinking beyond a single generation, we help frame the conversations — values, stewardship, and how wealth is meant to support those who come after — and translate them into a workable financial plan.

Our Approach

Our approach to estate & legacy

Legacy planning is patient work. It begins with understanding what matters to you and the people you intend to provide for — and only then turns to structure and mechanics.

As a fiduciary, we are obligated to put your interests first. That means we are candid about what falls inside our role and what does not. Ktema does not provide legal advice and does not draft wills, trusts, or other legal documents. Those instruments are prepared by your estate attorney, and we coordinate with that attorney so the financial plan and the legal plan reinforce one another.

Our part is to keep the financial picture clear and current: how accounts are titled, where beneficiary designations point, how gifts and charitable intentions fit your cash flow, and how the whole plan holds together as life changes. We revisit it as your circumstances and the law evolve, and we bring questions back to your attorney and tax advisor rather than guess at answers outside our lane.
